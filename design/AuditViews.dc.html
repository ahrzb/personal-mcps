Board notes
• Events merges what belongs together. Rows sharing a detail.approvalId are one chain row, headed by the approval.requested row but titled by the call it is the story of (home/set_scene, not approval.requested) — the sentence beneath already says an approval was asked for. It carries that sentence (“asked for approval → you approved 18:00 → ran ok 1.2 s”) and the state of its LAST event; the refused tools/call is the ask itself, not a step in it. Consecutive un-chained rows collapse to ×N runs on a seven-field signature — event, app, tool, principal, outcome, detail.failureClass and the arguments preview: a run is the SAME call repeated, so five searches with five different queries stay five rows, while the cron agent's refusals (which carry no arguments) still collapse. A chain never joins a run.
• WHEN is the time the row sorts by, which for a merged row is its newest event — not its head's. A chain row is titled by approval.requested, the chain's OLDEST event, and a run row by its newest member; printing the head's time on a chain made a newest-first list read as shuffled (an 07:47 chain landing between 11:47 and 08:07). The capture asserts it over every rendered Events list: a WHEN that steps forward going down fails the board.
• A run is a disclosure, not a summary that hides its evidence (owner, 2026-09-21: “the x5 runs look weird, how can I look at all of them? or look at the bodies?”). The ×N badge is a button (aria-expanded); beneath the title the row reads “N identical events · <first> → <last>”; opened, it lists its members newest first — time, duration, outcome chip — and each line opens its OWN record, bodies and all, 50 at a time behind Show more with Hide to collapse. The run's title still opens the newest member. The members sit inside the What happened cell, so the table's four columns hold. Which runs are open is reading position, not URL state — it does not belong in a link, and a reload starts closed.
• Only a call is titled like one. tools/call, prompts/get and resources/read are titled <app>/<tool>; every other event is titled by its event name with <app>/<tool> dim beside it — an approval.requested carries an app and a tool too, and titling it like a call makes the request and the refused call it caused read as the same row. The one exception is a chain row, which is one call’s story and takes that call’s title; the record it opens is still headed approval.requested home/set_scene. The rule holds in un-chained Events rows, the waterfall, the record head and the chain timeline.
• The third line is evidence, not decoration. argsHead clipped to 110 characters (an oversize stub renders as its placeholder), else the first three detail pairs — which is how a detail.approvalId or a failureClass is readable without opening the record. 120 rows at a time behind Load more; the foot counts rows against events.
• Sessions is salience, not completeness. Opening one draws the waterfall: runs of more than two ok tools/call rows fold to “N ok calls — apps”, everything else keeps its own line and opens its record. The right-hand text is the duration for an ok and the outcome class plus its failureClass or raw code otherwise.
• Brushing is not filtering. The brush is since/until applied client-side over the loaded rows and drawn ON the strip — the strip itself is never dimmed by it, because the point of the strip is to show what lies outside the selection.
